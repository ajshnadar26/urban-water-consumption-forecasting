# Methodology and limitations

The unit of analysis is one calendar month of selected Austin Water retail billing classes. Source rows are keyed by year-month, postal code and customer class. Exact repeated rows are removed; conflicting keys, invalid dates and nonfinite volumes stop processing. Negative billing adjustments remain in the raw data, and aggregated sector demands must be nonnegative. No missing sector months occur in this snapshot. If a sector month is missing in another snapshot, only causal forward filling is permitted; leading gaps require source repair.

All model selection uses 2012–2022 training observations and the 2023 validation year. The ADF test determines whether d=0 is supported; otherwise d=1 is checked. The current first difference rejects the unit-root null. Nine p,q pairs in 0..2 are fitted; converged models are ranked by training AIC. ACF/PACF show serial dependence but do not uniquely identify an order; the numerical search confirms the candidate choice. A wider search, seasonal ARIMA and rolling-origin evaluation are useful extensions.

Holt–Winters uses additive trend, damped trend and 12-month additive seasonality. Seasonal naive repeats the final 12 months. Weather/calendar regression uses an intercept, scaled time trend, annual sine/cosine, temperature, rainfall and month length. At each prediction origin, future weather inputs are calendar-month climatologies estimated only from the supplied history. Actual validation/test weather is never used to predict that same period. Calendar weekend counts are available for exploration but are not regression predictors. Population is unavailable; the time trend is not a measured population series.

After model selection, each method is refitted using observations through 2023 and predicts all 12 months of 2024 from one origin. There is no random split. The selected method stays fixed even if another model scores better on the test. Final forecasts refit through December 2024. Subsequent source records are deliberately excluded. Point forecasts and ARIMA interval bounds are clipped at zero for physical plausibility; this clipping changes nominal interval coverage near zero. No empirical interval coverage or calibrated intervals for alternative methods are claimed.

Anomaly screening uses the residual from the same month a year earlier. A median and median absolute deviation are computed from the previous 24 residuals, with at least 12 required. The robust score divides the centered error by 1.4826 times the MAD. Absolute scores greater than 3.5 are flagged. Zero-scale windows are unscored. Initial months form a warm-up period. This detector is separate from the offline STL visualization, which uses all history and is not a causal alert method. Valid flagged points remain in the model. Weather explanations are hypotheses, not incident diagnoses. No labeled incidents exist to measure precision or recall.

Total forecasts are split by the trailing 12-month observed sector shares. This preserves total demand but assumes stable sector composition. Sector-level models, uncertainty in shares, and postal-code-level prioritization would improve the system. Municipal billing is only a proxy for priority areas; real hospitals, vulnerable communities and statutory lifeline requirements need additional data.

Priority allocation solves a four-variable linear program. Floors, priority weights and capacities are assumed planning controls. Feasibility requires every floor to fit its sector cap and aggregate floors to fit supply. A proportional policy serves floors first and distributes remaining supply over eligible headroom. An infeasible case receives an explicit emergency fallback and minimum-service-violation column. Neither policy simulates pipe hydraulics, reservoir storage, pressure, pumping schedules or treatment constraints. Any real deployment needs those operational inputs and human approval.

MAE and RMSE are in million US gallons per month. MAPE uses only nonzero actuals and is not reported when every actual is zero. Twelve held-out months provide limited evidence: model rankings can change across years. Residual serial correlation, billing schedules, restrictions, weather extremes and changing population can all limit generalization.
